# Governance of YaskCode Academy

## Purpose

YaskCode Academy is an open-source educational initiative for learning Software Engineering by doing Software Engineering. It creates open, rigorous and reproducible learning experiences—especially for Spanish-speaking students, educators and communities—in Software Engineering, Artificial Intelligence and modern software-development practices.

This policy explains how the project is guided and evolves. It does not replace an institution's academic rules, and academic participation does not automatically become an accepted open-source contribution.

## Governance principles and scope

YaskCode Academy is **Spanish-first · English-accessible**: Spanish is the primary language for educational documentation and governance, while concise English references support international participation. The project is project-based, GitHub-native, standards-informed, evidence-based, iterative and open by design.

It maintains learning paths, educational resources, guides, practical projects, documentation, tools and collaboration spaces within its mission. References to CS50, GitHub Education, Open Source Collective or other initiatives are sources of inspiration, not official affiliations.

## Initial roles

- **Project Lead / Founding Maintainer:** in this founding stage, holds final responsibility for mission, educational quality, safety, licensing consistency, priorities and acceptance of changes.
- **Maintainers:** may review and maintain agreed areas when such responsibilities are formally assigned.
- **Contributors:** make accepted contributions such as educational resources, code, documentation, translations, accessibility work, review or testing.
- **Academic participants:** may take part in learning activities or private assessed work. That alone is not an accepted open-source contribution.

A person becomes a contributor when a contribution is accepted through the repository process, usually a Pull Request. Future maintainers will be invited based on sustained contributions, responsible review, relevant expertise, consistent conduct and willingness to take responsibility. There is no automatic promotion.

## Decisions and accountability

Issues and Pull Requests are the normal record for proposals, discussion and decisions. Minor, low-risk improvements may be accepted by a maintainer or the Project Lead. Significant changes—such as mission, learning model, licensing, academic-integrity policy, repository architecture, publication criteria, security or governance—need a traceable proposal and documented rationale.

The Project Lead retains final decision-making authority during the founding stage. This is intentional: the project will not claim distributed governance before it has the people and operating practice to sustain it. Material decisions are documented publicly when doing so does not expose personal data, protected academic information, vulnerabilities or other sensitive information.

Participants should disclose relevant personal, academic, financial or professional conflicts of interest and step back when appropriate. Disagreements are handled respectfully in the relevant work channel; unresolved matters are decided by the Project Lead with a short rationale. Conduct concerns follow the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## Evolution

Governance will be reviewed as sustained contribution, real maintainer responsibilities and demonstrated operational needs emerge. The direction is gradual, transparent distribution of responsibilities—not premature promises. The Spanish version, [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md), prevails if an interpretation differs.
